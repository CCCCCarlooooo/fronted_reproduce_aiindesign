A TURNING POINT

Last year, studios were piloting intelligent tools. This year, they’re rebuilding their practice around them.

Machine Craft 2026 charts how intelligent tooling is reshaping studio practice — at the desk, and across whole teams.

We started this study because one question kept coming up in every studio we visited: everyone is experimenting, but nobody can see what the room next door is doing. This report is our attempt to open those doors.

The findings draw on more than a thousand practitioners — independents, in-house teams, and agencies — working across product, brand, research, and engineering-adjacent roles. We paired the survey with long-form conversations with the people steering these changes.

Practice is moving faster than publishing cycles, so we’ll keep releasing field notes through the year, including visits with teams at Vertex, Halcyon, Oxbow, Plume, Cinder, and Marrow. Sign up for new releases.

ADA KOWALCZYK
STUDIO DIRECTOR, HALCYON

New instruments don’t replace the musician. They change what music gets written — and who gets to write it.

01
Tools

The stack
won’t sit still

Adoption has surged, but the toolbox keeps changing shape. Teams juggle twice as many off-the-shelf tools as last year, and many are quietly building their own utilities to fit how they like to work. With every release cycle the pace quickens — while dependable output quality remains the thing everyone is still chasing.

IN THIS CHAPTER, WE’LL COVER:

  • Which tools actually earn a seat
  • The rise of homegrown utilities
  • Budgets, seats, and shadow tooling
  • Why adoption stalls after the pilot
  • Keeping pace without burning out
Read the Tools Chapter→

02
Craft

Taste is the
new bottleneck

When a first draft costs minutes instead of days, the scarce skill shifts from production to judgment. Working prototypes are becoming the default way to show an idea, and more designers are shipping code they shaped themselves. At the same time, practitioners tell us they worry about skills going soft — and about how solitary the new workflow can feel.

IN THIS CHAPTER, WE’LL COVER:

  • Prototyping as the first draft
  • Code as a studio material
  • Speed, quality, and the space between
  • Training judgment, not just skills
  • From maker to conductor
Read the Craft Chapter→

03
Teams

Org charts
meet autonomy

The biggest shifts are happening between people, not inside tools. Leads are rewriting rituals, budgets, and role definitions on the fly, and the lines between design, product, and engineering keep smudging. The teams doing this well treat policy as a living document — written down after practice proves it, not before.

IN THIS CHAPTER, WE’LL COVER:

  • How leadership clears the runway
  • Roles are blurring at the edges
  • Rituals for messy collaboration
  • Policy that keeps up with practice
  • What hiring looks like now
